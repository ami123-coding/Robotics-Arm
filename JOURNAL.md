# October 4, 2026: Ctreated plan and base

I created the overall plan for this project and started on the bases, which the top and bottom are independent and slide in together so the electronics are easily accessibleI started off with modeling the whole system and determining where I would like to place my electronics. In order to keep my base easy to access in case I need to make changes later, I divided it into two distinct cylindrical segments.

I modeled the lower segment of the base first and then designed the upper portion so that it can slide over or fit onto the lower part. I modified both the diameter and the wall thickness of the segments slightly so that they would fit properly while keeping the base compact.

I added an opening to the upper part of the base for wires to pass through from the electronic components and to the rotating segment. Overall, this session was focused on designing the proper proportions of the base along with sufficient inner space for the electronics later.

At the end of this session, I got the two-part base completed and determined the overall proportions of the other segments of the arm.

# October 4, 2026: added servo1. holder

just added the servo for and the holder on the bases, found the 3d models for the servos and made holders for those measurements, cut holes for wires and aligned everything perfectly. I moved on to installing the first servo on the base. Instead of making an assumption about the dimensions of the servo and making the holder accordingly, I found the 3D model of the servo prior to designing the holder.

I imported the model of the servo into Onshape and installed it to the upper part of the base. Next, I created the custom holder that was supposed to be holding the body of the servo firmly but allowing for the operation of the output shaft at the same time.

I made adjustments to the holder of the servo housing, adding some room for the servo itself to fit into, not hitting the walls of the holder by accident. I have also created holes in the holder so that the wires of the servo could be routed into the base for further connection to the electronics.

I spent some time aligning the servo output with the center of the base since this particular servo is going to rotate the arm assembly.

# October 5, 2026: added revolving and shoulder servo and revolving plate

I added the revolving plate allowing the arm to move in a circle and also added the shoulder joint that will 1. hold the weight of the whole arm and 2. provide the arm with the greatest axis of motion,The rotating plate was installed to enable the arm to rotate around the base, and the shoulder joint was also added. The reason why the shoulder was so important was due to its necessity to support almost all the weight of the arm and provide one of the broadest range of motions.

At first, I worked on the base rotation servo holder. Additional slots were made for wires and then the circular servo horn was attached to the output of the base rotation servo. To enable the horn to attach directly to the rotating plate, it was integrated into the design of the rotating plate rather than installing the plate on top.

This step required precise positioning of the horn, servo shaft, and the rotating plate itself. Otherwise, it was impossible to assure that the horn, shaft, and plate are aligned with each other and the deviation of even the slightest amount could cause an uneven spinning of the upper arm around the base.

Next came the design of the rotating plate itself and constant optimization of its diameter and thickness to have enough space for the shoulder assembly, but to leave some gap from the stationary base as well to prevent rubbing against it during its rotation.

The holder of the shoulder servo was created on the top of the rotating plate. It was made to be able to hold the servo model within, and all the dimensions of the holder, as well as spacing and positioning of the servo body and output shaft, were modified constantly.

There was also enough space left for movement, wires, and airflow, which consumed much of the two hours.

# October 6, 2026: added more arm segments and servos

I basically finished up the arm segments and servo placements for the mobility of the arm itself. It took way longer than expected. I custom-made 3 more pairs of arm segments with spacing for the respective servos and attached the servos, allowing the arm to reach out far and have 3 degrees of motion, as well as a rotating base, for a current total of 4 servos.This is probably one of the most tedious but still fun steps in the design process since I had to manually design pretty much all of the side pieces of the main arm linkages, which do not have rectangular shape. I wanted to make the arm as light as possible by using custom-made shapes and huge cutouts in order to minimize the filament usage and keep the weight low, but still leave enough material in the mounting locations.

for the joints, I had to design the pieces of the arm with the consideration of the dimensions of the specific servos used for those particular joints. I designed several custom-made servo holders and mounting points in order to allow the rigidity and proper attachment of the following arm linkages. As every joint had a little bit different spacing, I had to work on the widths, clearances, and mounting points quite a bit.

Additionally, I created custom pieces of arm to make a solid mount for the claw rotating servo, which had to hold it in place and prevent from shifting in any direction. 

while assembling the arm I realized that I also needed to create a decent route for the wires. I came back and redesigned the arm peices in order to provide the passages for the wires without any interference and risk of getting pinch-blocked.

At this session, I mounted and positioned all of the main arm servos. I checked the assembled part of the arm with the mates, moved the joints, and fixed all of the parts that got too close to each other.

# October 7, 2026: claw mech basic and claw roating mech

added tow more servos, one to rotate the claw 360 and one to actually move the claw, created a new set of arm, as well as a placment holder and reinforcements for the claw rotating servo and mounted it,Probably the most challenging piece to design was the claw. Because the master Onshape file became so large as to cause my computer to slow down, I had to save the claw in its own separate file and then import the assembled version back into the master file.

I tried a bunch of claw designs, although I only managed to save screenshots of the final one. In the very beginning, the design was more straightforward in that the servo horn would directly move one pincher of the claw, which was connected to the other pincher.

After a bit I switched to a gear design. The gear teeth were actually carved into the claw arms in such a way that by turning one side of the claws with the servo, I could make the other side turn in the opposite direction. Thus, both sides of the claw would be able to move together with the use of just one servo.

In the end, the two sides of the claw were joined into one bigger claw, additional support to the pivots opposite of the servo holder was added, as well as mounting to the housing. I chose this design because it would allow for more grip and allow the claw to pick up bigger things, the gears on the claw were made direcvttly onto the claw picnhers and replecated over and i made them bevel style for less chance of them slipping and wearing down since it is 3d prinetd after all, using a gear relation and putting them on the servo box mount allowed them to move in synchronizaion, I then pulled the claw from the document i craeted to the main doucment, . before I mounted it however I wasnted to make it so the claw spins so i used the same mechanism I used for the very base and using  the mounted sevo that was on the edge of the arm from before, used a servo horn to connect it to a rotating plate and then I attached the claw

# October 10, 2026: Finished!

This session was for cleaning up and tweaking the robot arm CAD as the last step to finish. During my checks and improvements, I added fillets to many parts that had sharp edges in order to improve their appearance and printing process.

However, while checking the assembly once more time, I noticed that there was not enough space for the wires in most of the servo casings and arm parts. Therefore, I had to do a slight redesign to mostly all if not many parts that were already ready and revise them.

I made new openings for the wires and adjusted the clearances in the servo holders and arm parts so that the cables had enough space to route without getting pinched or blocking anything. After these steps, I have checked the assembly again and finalized the clearances and shapes of some of the parts. Then I just moved it around and set limits to all the mates, animated them to make sure nothing was blocking other movements, tested the degrees of freedom and then finished!
